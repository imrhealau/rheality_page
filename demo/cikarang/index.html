Demo · Cikarang, Indonesia · live archive, null result

Eight data centers, two years, nothing moving

Cikarang, east of Jakarta, holds most of Indonesia's new data center capacity, on the same alluvial plain that has made North Jakarta one of the fastest sinking cities on record. We measured the ground under eight data centers from the free Sentinel-1 radar archive: 61 satellite passes between September 2024 and September 2026, 119 interferograms, sampled at building coordinates taken from PeeringDB and OpenStreetMap.

Every one of them is flat to within our own measurement noise. Find your site in the table below. The full working, including a mistake we made and corrected in public, is further down the page.

What a null result is worth

Reading this the way an operator would

Nothing here clears its own error bar, and that is the finding. Free radar over two years puts the ground under all eight halls inside a few mm/yr of each other and of zero. An operator who was about to commission a ground survey on a hunch now has an ordering: none of these eight, and a subsiding area 6 km north east that would matter a great deal to anyone building in it.

What it is not is a certificate. Free radar over two years can tell flat ground from moving ground, but on tropical ground it cannot give an exact rate. When we checked our own rates against GNSS stations on tropical ground in Puerto Rico over six months, they were off by 54 mm/yr RMS, which spread across two years is still of order 20 mm/yr on an absolute rate. Nothing in the table clears that bar either, which is why this reads as a null and not as eight precise measurements.

The correction · why coordinates come first

An estate centroid is not a building

The first version of this study sampled one point per industrial estate. The point for Kota Jababeka, an estate of about 5,600 hectares, read 23 mm/yr faster sinking than its neighbours, and we named the estate's data center tenant beside the number. That point sits 6.2 km from the nearest verified data hall, on the flank of a regional subsidence area. The ground at that point is moving, and no data center is on it. How much of that area falls inside the estate boundary is something we cannot see from outside.

The middle button on the chart puts that series back. It is the same pipeline, the same stack and the same inversion, sampled 6 km away, and it is a 23 mm/yr difference in the headline. That is why every study now starts from the coordinate of the building.
